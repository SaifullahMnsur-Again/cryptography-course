01. Caesar Cipher

Shift-based substitution where each alphabetic character moves by a fixed key.

$$ \begin{aligned} E_k(x) &= (x + k) \bmod 26 \\ D_k(x) &= (x - k) \bmod 26 \end{aligned} $$
Mathematical algorithm
$$ \begin{aligned} &\text{Index map: } A \mapsto 0,\,B \mapsto 1,\,\dots,\,Z \mapsto 25,\quad x \in \mathbb{Z}_{26} \\ &\text{Given } k \in \mathbb{Z}_{26} \\ &E_k(x) = (x+k) \bmod 26,\qquad D_k(x) = (x-k) \bmod 26 \\ &\forall c \notin \{A\dots Z, a\dots z\}:\ E_k(c)=c,\ D_k(c)=c \end{aligned} $$

04. Playfair Cipher

Digraph cipher with a 5x5 matrix (I/J merged). Encrypt by row, column, or rectangle rules.

$$ \begin{aligned} r_1=r_2&:\ (c_1,c_2)\mapsto(c_1+1,c_2+1)\bmod 5 \\ c_1=c_2&:\ (r_1,r_2)\mapsto(r_1+1,r_2+1)\bmod 5 \\ ext{else}&:\ (r_1,c_1),(r_2,c_2)\mapsto(r_1,c_2),(r_2,c_1) \end{aligned} $$
Mathematical algorithm
$$ \begin{aligned} &M\in\Sigma^{5\times 5},\ \Sigma=\{A,\dots,Z\}\setminus\{J\},\ J\mapsto I \\ &\text{Digraph prep: } (a,b),\ a=b\Rightarrow (a,X,b),\ |P|\text{ odd}\Rightarrow X\text{ pad} \\ &\operatorname{pos}(a)=(r_1,c_1),\ \operatorname{pos}(b)=(r_2,c_2) \\ &r_1=r_2:\ (a,b)\mapsto(M_{r_1,(c_1+1)\bmod 5},\ M_{r_2,(c_2+1)\bmod 5}) \\ &c_1=c_2:\ (a,b)\mapsto(M_{(r_1+1)\bmod 5,c_1},\ M_{(r_2+1)\bmod 5,c_2}) \\ &\text{otherwise: } (a,b)\mapsto(M_{r_1,c_2},\ M_{r_2,c_1}) \end{aligned} $$

05. Hill Cipher (2x2)

Linear algebra based cipher with modulo 26 matrix multiplication.

$$ \begin{aligned} C &= KP \pmod{26} \\ P &= K^{-1}C \pmod{26} \\ \gcd(\det K,26)&=1 \end{aligned} $$
Mathematical algorithm
$$ \begin{aligned} &K=\begin{bmatrix}k_{00}&k_{01}\\k_{10}&k_{11}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{Z}_{26}^{2\times2} \\ &\det(K)=k_{00}k_{11}-k_{01}k_{10}\pmod{26} \\ &\text{Valid }\Leftrightarrow \gcd(\det(K),26)=1 \\ &C\equiv KP\pmod{26},\quad P\equiv K^{-1}C\pmod{26} \\ &K^{-1}\equiv (\det K)^{-1}\,\operatorname{adj}(K)\pmod{26} \end{aligned} $$

06. Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange

Both parties derive the same shared secret without sending their private keys.

$$ \begin{aligned} A &= g^a \bmod p \\ B &= g^b \bmod p \\ S_A &= B^a \bmod p \\ S_B &= A^b \bmod p \end{aligned} $$
Mathematical algorithm
$$ \begin{aligned} &\text{Public: } p\text{ prime},\ g\in\mathbb{Z}_p^* \\ &\text{Alice private }a,\ \text{Bob private }b \\ &A\equiv g^a\pmod p,\quad B\equiv g^b\pmod p \\ &S_A\equiv B^a\equiv (g^b)^a\equiv g^{ab}\pmod p \\ &S_B\equiv A^b\equiv (g^a)^b\equiv g^{ab}\pmod p \\ &\therefore\ S_A=S_B \end{aligned} $$

07. Block Cipher Modes (ECB, CBC, CFB, OFB, CTR)

Your C++ files use XOR as block encryption primitive. This simulator keeps the same logic and visualizes each mode chain.

$$ \begin{aligned} ext{Block size} &= 8\ \text{bytes} \\ E_K(X) &= X \oplus K \end{aligned} $$
Mathematical algorithm
$$ \begin{aligned} &E_K(X)=X\oplus K \\ &\text{ECB: } C_i=E_K(P_i) \\ &\text{CBC: } C_i=E_K(P_i\oplus C_{i-1}),\ C_0=IV \\ &\text{CFB: } C_i=P_i\oplus \operatorname{MSB}_s(E_K(R_i)),\ R_{i+1}=\operatorname{shift}(R_i,C_i) \\ &\text{OFB: } O_i=E_K(O_{i-1}),\ O_0=Nonce,\ C_i=P_i\oplus O_i \\ &\text{CTR: } C_i=P_i\oplus E_K(CTR_i),\ CTR_{i+1}=CTR_i+1 \\ &\text{For CFB/OFB/CTR, decryption uses the same }\oplus\text{ form.} \end{aligned} $$

08. Elliptic Curve Diffie-Hellman (ECDH)

Same key exchange idea as DH, but over elliptic curve points with scalar multiplication.

$$ \begin{aligned} E: y^2 &\equiv x^3+ax+b \pmod p \\ P_{pub} &= n_{priv}\,G \\ S &= n_{priv}\,P_{other} \end{aligned} $$
Mathematical algorithm
$$ \begin{aligned} &E(\mathbb{F}_p):\ y^2\equiv x^3+ax+b\pmod p,\quad G\in E(\mathbb{F}_p) \\ &\text{Alice private }n_A,\ \text{Bob private }n_B \\ &P_A=n_A G,\quad P_B=n_B G \\ &S_A=n_A P_B=n_A(n_B G) \\ &S_B=n_B P_A=n_B(n_A G) \\ &\therefore\ S_A=S_B=(n_A n_B)G \end{aligned} $$
